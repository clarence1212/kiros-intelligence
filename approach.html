Our approach

The Agent Value Ledger

Every agent must earn its place in the accounts. We start from your financial statements, not the technology, and hold each agent to the line it is meant to move.

Why it matters

Agents are being deployed at speed, often because competitors are deploying them. Pilots multiply, running costs rise, and boards are left asking a simple question few can answer: what has this done to our results?

We think that question should be answered before an agent is built, not after. The Agent Value Ledger reads AI through the lens leaders already use to run the business, their financial statements, and treats every agent as an investment that has to show a return.

It brings strategy, finance and audit together. Our partners read accounts as well as architectures, and we measure results with the discipline of an assurance engagement.

Four lines

What every agent must answer to

An agent that cannot be tied to at least one of these lines is an experiment, not an investment.

01

Top line

Revenue

Conversion, pricing, retention, share of wallet and new products that agents make possible.

02

Costs

Operating cost and cost of revenue

Hours, handoffs and rework taken out of a process, net of what it costs to run and supervise the agent.

03

Capital

Working capital and capital spend

Cash released from receivables, inventory and payables, and capital that no longer needs to be spent.

04

Risk

Losses, provisions and compliance cost

Fewer losses, errors and fraud, and lower cost to meet regulatory obligations, counted only when the evidence is in.

The method

Five steps from the accounts to the agent

  1. 01

    Read the accounts

    We start with the annual report, management accounts and segment results, and compare them with listed peers. Where does the business trail its sector, and which lines carry the most weight?

  2. 02

    Map the value levers

    For each line that matters, we trace the operating drivers behind it and the decisions inside those drivers. Only then do we ask where an agent could move the number.

  3. 03

    Build a case for every agent

    Each proposed agent gets a one-page case: the line it moves, today's baseline, the expected effect, the full cost of running it, a named owner and the conditions under which it is switched off.

  4. 04

    Decide and sequence

    Cases are ranked as a portfolio, by value, time to evidence and risk, and each agent is placed on the delegation map: act, recommend, or inform only.

  5. 05

    Measure and assure

    After deployment, results are measured against the baseline with evidence an auditor would accept, and reported to the board as a quarterly ledger. Agents that do not earn their place are stopped.

Our discipline

Rules we hold to

Capacity is not a saving

Freed hours count only when they are redeployed to revenue work or replace hiring that would otherwise happen.

Count the full cost

Model usage, integration, data work, human oversight, validation and assurance all go on the ledger.

No baseline, no agent

If a result cannot be measured before deployment, it cannot be claimed after.

Risk benefits wait for evidence

Lower losses or fewer incidents are counted only once there is enough history to show them.

Nothing to sell

We do not resell agents, platforms or licences, so we can tell you when not to deploy one.
