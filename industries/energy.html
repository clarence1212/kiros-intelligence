The issue

Property owners, facilities operators and energy companies now run assets that software can monitor and control: buildings that adjust their own energy use, batteries that trade with the grid, and maintenance scheduled on condition rather than by the calendar.

At the same time, AI's own electricity demand is reshaping capital plans across Asia-Pacific. Regulators in the EU, the US and Australia are tightening rules on AI in critical infrastructure, and cyber agencies have issued joint principles for AI in operational technology.

Common industry challenges
  • Rising operating costs per square metre and per MWh
  • Ageing assets and large capital plans
  • Sustainability reporting and energy-efficiency obligations
  • Operational-technology security as more assets connect
Typical readiness barriers
  • Building and asset data locked in separate systems
  • Operational technology that is hard to connect safely
  • Contracts that leave flexibility value with third parties
  • Limited in-house data and analytics capability
