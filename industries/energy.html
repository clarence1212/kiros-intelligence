Utilities, smart buildings, grids and AI's own energy demand

Energy & sustainability

AI is the largest new source of power demand and one of the best tools for managing it.

The issue

Grid operators are already running agents in live control-room work, and software increasingly decides when household batteries, buildings and data centres charge, export or curtail. Margin is moving towards whoever controls those flexible assets.

At the same time, AI's own electricity demand is reshaping capital plans across Asia-Pacific. Regulators in the EU, the US and Australia are tightening rules on AI in critical infrastructure, and cyber agencies have issued joint principles for AI in operational technology.

Questions we help leaders answer

What boards are asking

  1. Which operational decisions have we delegated to agents, and who signs off the limits on each?
  2. Could any of our AI systems count as a high-risk safety component under the EU AI Act?
  3. In the contest to control flexible energy assets, are we the orchestrator or the asset being orchestrated?
  4. Have we tested our AI-enabled operational technology against the joint international principles?

Handing building and grid decisions to agents

Our approach. Clear limits on what the agent decides, operational-technology security tested against international principles, and a commercial model that keeps the value with the owner.
