Education & families

AI tutors are going national. Assessment and child safety have to keep up.

The issue

Governments are funding AI tutors at national scale, while AI agents can now complete whole online courses on a student's behalf. The evidence shows that tools designed for learning help, and general chatbots can leave students worse off once the AI is removed.

Regulation centred on children is already in force in Australia, the UK and the US, and the EU's rules for education AI follow in December 2027. Schools, edtech providers and care organisations need to decide where AI may act, for whom, and under whose supervision.

Questions we help leaders answer

What boards are asking

  1. Which decisions about learners or care recipients do we let AI make, and who can override them?
  2. If an agent could complete our unsupervised assessments, which credentials still prove what a student can do?
  3. Do our vendor contracts cover age assurance, data use and training on student data?
  4. Are our most disadvantaged learners getting the best AI provision, or the cheapest?

AI tutors and assessment that still mean something

Situation. A school network wants an AI tutor for every student, while teachers are finding coursework completed by agents.

Our approach. An age-staged delegation map, vendor standards for age assurance and data use, and assessment redesigned around supervised checks.
