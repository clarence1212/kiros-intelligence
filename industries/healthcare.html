The issue

Regulators, health systems and pharmaceutical companies are already using agents for chart review, scheduling, prior authorisation and medical enquiries. That work sits right next to clinical judgement, which is where regulators are now looking.

Expectations are converging across the FDA, EMA, Singapore's MOH and HSA, and Australia's TGA: a defined context of use, lifecycle monitoring and named accountability. Organisations that redesign accountability before they scale will get the gains without the hidden risk.

Common industry challenges
  • Workforce shortages and high agency and overtime costs
  • Revenue leakage from coding errors and claim denials
  • Bed capacity and patient flow
  • Clinical safety and regulation as AI moves closer to care
Typical readiness barriers
  • Clinical data across many systems with strict access rules
  • Clinician time to design and test new workflows
  • The line between administrative and regulated clinical use
  • Privacy and consent requirements

Hypothetical scenario · Healthcare & aged care

Agents inside the clinical record

Situation. A hospital group's agents prepare patient summaries and discharge paperwork. Departments now want their own, and some agents are drifting towards clinical suggestions.

Our approach. An agent register, a clear line between administrative and clinical use, drift monitoring with authority to suspend, and governance built to the strictest market's standard.

What good looks like. Departments build within clear limits, and regulators in every market see one consistent approach.
