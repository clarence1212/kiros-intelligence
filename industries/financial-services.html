The issue

Banks, insurers and wealth managers have moved from AI that drafts and summarises to agents that carry out multi-step work: assembling credit memos, handling claims and completing customer requests.

Regulators in Singapore, Hong Kong, Europe and the US are moving in step, and every one of them keeps accountability with the institution, including for models bought from vendors. The advantage will go to firms that redesign delegation, accountability and controls before the rules are set for them.

Common industry challenges
  • Cost-to-income and expense ratios under pressure from competitors with lighter operating models
  • Rising compliance cost, from KYC refresh to regulatory reporting
  • Advisers and relationship managers spending too little time with clients
  • New supervisory expectations for AI, including inventories, materiality and third-party AI
Typical readiness barriers
  • Fragmented customer and product data across legacy systems
  • Model risk frameworks that do not yet cover agents
  • Vendor AI embedded in software without an inventory
  • Unclear approval thresholds for agent actions

Hypothetical scenario · Financial services & investment management

Governing a bank's first credit agents

Situation. A mid-sized bank's pilot agent drafts credit memos well. Leaders want it rolled out within six months, but nobody can say exactly what it may do or who answers when it is wrong.

Our approach. A delegation map with approval thresholds by loan size, an agent register with named owners, a plan to suspend or replace the vendor model, and a workforce plan for the roles that change.

What good looks like. The bank scales the agent with board approval and a governance model it can show its regulator.
