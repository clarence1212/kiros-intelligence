Manufacturing, logistics & supply chains

Agents now plan, buy and move goods, and robots are entering the factory floor.

The issue

Agents are moving from pilots into planning, procurement and logistics, and physical AI is entering factories and warehouses. The gains are real, but so is the risk of agents making spending and scheduling decisions without clear limits.

Margins in manufacturing and logistics are thin, so the discipline of the ledger matters: scrap, inventory days, cost per shipment and service penalties are where results show up.

Common industry challenges
  • Thin margins and volatile input costs
  • Inventory and working capital tied up across the network
  • Labour shortages in operations and warehousing
  • Service penalties for late or incomplete delivery
Typical readiness barriers
  • Operational data spread across plants and partners
  • Planning systems that are hard to integrate
  • Agents buying or scheduling without spending caps
  • Entry-level planner roles at risk

Hypothetical scenario · Manufacturing, logistics & supply chains

Planning agents without limits

Situation. A manufacturer's planning agents reorder materials and reschedule lines automatically, but nobody has set spending caps or agreed who can override them.

Our approach. Delegation policies with caps by value and supplier, a ledger for each agent, and a plan for the planner roles that change.

What good looks like. The agents keep their speed, and every material decision has a limit and an owner.
